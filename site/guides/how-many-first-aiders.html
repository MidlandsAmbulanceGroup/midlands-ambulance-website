How many first aiders do I need at my event?

The factors that decide the size of your medical team, and how the Purple Guide, the Event Safety Guide and health and safety law shape the answer.

There is no single ratio that works for every event. The right level of cover depends on who is coming, what they will be doing and how quickly help can reach them.

What the law says

The Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and the Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999 require event organisers to carry out a suitable and sufficient risk assessment, and to put the right measures in place. Medical and first aid cover is part of that. The Health and Safety (First-Aid) Regulations 1981 also apply to your own staff and crew.

What the guidance says

The HSE's Event Safety Guide (HSG195) gave suggested first aid numbers based on attendance. Its successor, the Purple Guide, takes a risk-based approach instead: organisers assess the event and plan medical, ambulance and first aid resources to match. Councils and Safety Advisory Groups usually expect to see that reasoning written down.

What decides the level of cover

  • Attendance, including peak numbers on site at once.
  • The activity. A motorsport day carries different risks to a craft fair.
  • The audience. Age, vulnerability, alcohol and the likely mood of the crowd.
  • The site. Size, terrain, stages and how spread out it is.
  • Duration and weather. Long, hot or overnight events need more.
  • Distance to hospital and how busy local NHS services are.

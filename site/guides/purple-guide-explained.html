The Purple Guide explained for event organisers

What the Purple Guide is, who uses it and what it means for your medical cover.

The Purple Guide is the UK events industry's guidance on health, safety and welfare at music and other outdoor events. Councils and Safety Advisory Groups often use it as their reference point.

What it covers

Planning, crowd management, welfare, and the medical, ambulance and first aid section that most organisers ask us about.

What it means for medical cover

It sets out a risk-based way to decide what medical resources an event needs, and expects organisers to plan and record that decision. Attendance matters, but so do the type of event, the audience, the site, the duration and how far the nearest hospital is.

What organisers need to do

  • Carry out a medical needs assessment as part of your event risk assessment.
  • Book a competent medical provider with the right grades of staff.
  • Write down your medical arrangements in a medical plan.
  • Agree how the medical team links with event control, security and welfare.
  • Plan for a major incident at larger events.

Where it came from

It replaced the HSE's Event Safety Guide (HSG195), which was first published as the original "Purple Guide". The newer guide moved away from fixed numbers towards planning medical cover from a risk assessment.

Do I have to follow it?

It is guidance rather than law, but it describes what good practice looks like. The law itself, the Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and its regulations, requires a suitable and sufficient risk assessment. Following the Purple Guide helps show you took reasonable steps.

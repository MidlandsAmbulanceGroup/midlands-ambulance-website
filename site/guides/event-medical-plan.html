Planning

What goes in an event medical plan?

The sections a Safety Advisory Group expects to see, in plain English.

A medical plan explains how injured or unwell people at your event will be found, treated and, if needed, handed over to the NHS.

What it usually includes

  • Event details, timings and expected attendance.
  • The risk assessment behind your medical resources.
  • Staff numbers, grades and positions.
  • Vehicles, equipment and the treatment area.
  • Communication with event control.
  • Arrangements with local NHS services and hospitals.
  • Major incident procedures.

Who writes it

We write the plan for every booking, so you can share it with your Safety Advisory Group and insurers.

Who reads it

Your Safety Advisory Group, licensing authority, venue and insurer may all ask for it. The local NHS ambulance service may also want to know your arrangements for larger events.

When to start

As early as you can. Many Safety Advisory Groups want your event management plan, including the medical plan, weeks before the event. Tell us your deadline when you ask for a quote.

Common gaps

  • No clear access route for an ambulance to reach the treatment area.
  • No plan for what happens if a patient needs hospital.
  • Staff numbers with no explanation of how they were decided.
  • No link between the medical team and event control.

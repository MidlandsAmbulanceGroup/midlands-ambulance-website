Privacy policy

Last updated: October 2026

This policy explains what personal information Midlands Ambulance Group Ltd collects, why we collect it, how we look after it and what your rights are. It covers our website, enquiries and quotes, the patients we treat at events, people who attend our training courses, and people who apply to work with us. It is written to meet the UK General Data Protection Regulation (UK GDPR), the Data Protection Act 2018, the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 and the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations (PECR).

Who we are

Midlands Ambulance Group Ltd is the "controller" of your personal information. That means we decide how and why it is used and we are responsible for protecting it.

What we collect and why

Enquiries and quotes. When you ask for a quote or contact us, we collect your name, organisation, email, phone number and the event details you give us, such as dates, venue, expected attendance and any risk assessment you upload. We use this to reply, plan your medical cover and prepare a quote. Our lawful basis is taking steps at your request before entering into a contract, and then performing that contract.

Patients we treat. If we assess or treat you at an event or on site, we record your name, date of birth, contact details, what happened, your condition, observations, the treatment we gave and who we handed your care to. Some of this is health information, which the law treats as special category data. We record it to give you safe care, to hand over to the NHS or another clinician, and to keep a proper clinical record. Our lawful bases are:

  • legitimate interests and, where it applies, protecting your vital interests (UK GDPR Article 6(1)(f) and 6(1)(d));
  • for health information, the provision of health care by or under the responsibility of a health professional (Article 9(2)(h) and Schedule 1, Part 1, paragraph 2 of the Data Protection Act 2018), or protecting your vital interests when you cannot consent (Article 9(2)(c)).

Training courses. When you book or attend a course, we collect your name, contact details, employer (if they are paying), attendance, assessment results and certificate details. If you tell us about a health condition or access need so we can support you, we record that too, with your consent. We use this information to run the course, issue certificates and answer later questions about whether a certificate is valid. Our lawful bases are contract and, for any health or access information, your explicit consent.

Job applicants, staff and volunteers. When you apply to join our team, we collect your CV, registration details (for example HCPC or NMC), qualifications, references, right-to-work documents and, where the role needs it, DBS check results. We use this to assess your application and to meet our legal duties as an employer. Our lawful bases are contract, legal obligation and legitimate interests. For criminal record information, we rely on the employment and safeguarding conditions in Schedule 1 of the Data Protection Act 2018.

Clients and suppliers. We keep contact details, contracts and invoices for the organisations we work with. We do this to manage the contract and to meet tax and accounting law.

Marketing. We only send marketing emails if you have opted in, or if you are an existing client and we are telling you about similar services. Every email lets you unsubscribe, and you can opt out at any time.

Website visits. Our web host automatically records technical information when you visit, such as your IP address, browser type and the pages you view. This keeps the site secure and working. Our lawful basis is legitimate interests.

Cookies

Our website only uses cookies and similar technologies that are strictly necessary to deliver the site and the forms you choose to fill in. We do not use advertising or tracking cookies.

The website loads its fonts from Google Fonts. This means your browser shares your IP address with Google when a page loads.

If we add website analytics in future, we will update this policy and give you a way to opt out before any analytics run.

Who we share it with

We do not sell your personal information. We only share it when we need to:

  • NHS services and other clinicians taking over your care, such as an NHS ambulance service, hospital or your GP.
  • Event organisers, limited to what they need for their safety and incident records. For example, we would tell them that someone was treated and taken to hospital, but not your clinical details unless you agree or the law requires it.
  • Regulators, the police, coroners, courts and safeguarding teams where the law requires it, or where someone is at risk of harm.
  • Your employer, if they booked and paid for your training, so they can see your attendance and certificate.
  • Our insurers and professional advisers, such as accountants and solicitors.
  • Service providers who handle data for us under contract and only on our instructions. This includes our website host (Wix), our online forms provider (Cognito Forms), our email and file storage provider (Google Workspace) and our accounting software (QuickBooks).

Transfers outside the UK

Some of our service providers store data in the European Economic Area, Israel or the United States. When your information leaves the UK, we make sure it is protected. That protection is either UK adequacy regulations for the destination country, or the UK International Data Transfer Agreement or Addendum, or the UK Extension to the EU-US Data Privacy Framework.

How long we keep it

  • Patient records: adults, 8 years after the treatment. Children, until their 25th birthday, or their 26th if they were 17 when treated. Both periods follow the NHS Records Management Code of Practice.
  • Training records and certificates: 6 years after the course ends.
  • Enquiries and quotes that don't go ahead: 2 years.
  • Client contracts and invoices: 6 years after the end of the financial year they relate to.
  • Unsuccessful job applications: 6 months after the decision.
  • Staff records: 6 years after employment ends.

We may keep records longer if there is a legal claim, an investigation or a safeguarding concern. When we no longer need information, we delete or destroy it securely.

Your rights

You have the right to:

  • ask for a copy of the personal information we hold about you;
  • ask us to correct anything that is wrong or incomplete;
  • ask us to delete your information. Note that we usually have to keep clinical records for the periods above;
  • ask us to restrict how we use your information;
  • object to us using your information on the basis of legitimate interests, and to all direct marketing;
  • ask us to send information you gave us to you or to another organisation (data portability), where this applies;
  • withdraw your consent at any time, where we rely on consent. Withdrawing consent does not affect anything we did before you withdrew it.

We do not make decisions about you by purely automated means.

To use any of these rights, email us or call us using the details above. We may need to confirm your identity first. We will reply within one month. For complex requests the law allows two further months, and if we need that time we will tell you within the first month. There is normally no charge.

How we protect it

We use encrypted devices and secure, access-controlled storage. Paper patient records are kept secure on scene and transferred to locked storage. Only staff who need information can see it. Everyone on our team is trained in confidentiality and data protection. If a breach is likely to put you at risk, we will tell you and the ICO as the law requires.

Complaints

If you are unhappy with how we have handled your personal information, please tell us first. Email info@midlandsambulance.co.uk with "Data protection complaint" in the subject line, call 0333 335 8999, or write to our registered office. We will acknowledge your complaint within 30 days, look into it without undue delay and tell you the outcome.

You also have the right to complain to the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) at any time:

  • Website: ico.org.uk/make-a-complaint
  • Helpline: 0303 123 1113
  • Post: Information Commissioner's Office, Wycliffe House, Water Lane, Wilmslow, Cheshire SK9 5AF

Changes to this policy

We review this policy at least once a year and whenever the law or our services change. The date at the top shows when it was last updated. If we make a significant change, we will tell people affected where we can.
